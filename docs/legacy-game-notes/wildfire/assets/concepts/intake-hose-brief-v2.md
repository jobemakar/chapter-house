# Wildfire — modular intake and hose concept notes

Created with the built-in image generation tool, using blocked-creek-v1.png as the edit target. The prior concept is preserved. This changes concept art only, not the playable demo.

## Gameplay direction

- Carve/clear soil to route water into an intake. A separate matching hose sprays the fire. Intake and nozzle share green/brass materials and cyan droplet emblems; use an introductory animation or matching pulse in the game to teach the causal relationship.
- Three collectible canteen buddies per level: water delivered within their trigger radius fills a window and activates a smile/glow. Award only once per canteen. Positions, trigger radius, collected state, and water needed are level data. Optional branches provide collectible tradeoffs.
- Canteens are an original thematic survival collectible, not a claimed recurring item from the book. Alternatives: smiling compass buddies (finding a way to safety), or pinecone pals (cute forest mascots, less book-specific).
- Assumed book: Wildfire by Rodman Philbrick. Publisher synopsis establishes Sam and Delphy's wilderness escape and survival themes: https://www.scholastic.ca/our-books/book/wildfire-9781338266917

## Buildable art structure

Treat this as a target direction, not a ready-to-use tile atlas. Production assets should be a small reusable set: soil fills, grass top edges, inside/outside corners, transition pieces, grey stone obstacle modules, water source sprite, intake sprite, nozzle sprite, flame animation, and one canteen sprite with empty/full variants. Levels can be authored on an internal grid plus placed entities, but artwork must read as a continuous natural environment, not a visible grid of square blocks. Forest decoration stays outside collision geometry. Excavation can stay smooth within modular terrain. Water remains a dynamic simulation/render layer. The campsite/hose/fire scene is reusable and is not locked to a bottom strip.

## User-confirmed layout and modularity rules

These rules supersede the layout restrictions in the historical v2 image prompt below. The existing concept is one example level, not a mandatory level template.

- Terrain/playable regions do not need to span the full horizontal width. One level may use the full width; another may use only part of it. Allow compact, offset, irregular, separated, and mixed-size regions with surrounding scenery or open space.
- Intakes are not limited to an upward-facing pipe at the bottom. They may enter from the side or occupy other suitable positions and orientations. Intake placement and orientation are per-level data.
- Support multiple intakes in a level, including dummy/decoy intakes. Their role and hose/target association must be per-entity data, not hard-coded to one intake and one fixed destination. Exact dummy behavior and how players recognize a dummy remain design decisions.
- The hose outlet and fire scene can be spatially disconnected from the intake. Teach the association through consistent matching visual language and feedback; do not require a visible connecting pipe or fixed screen placement.
- "Tile-based" means modular and easy for Codex to assemble/reconfigure, not artwork that looks tiled. Avoid mandatory visible seams, checkerboards, or repeated square-rock faces. Use seamless fills, edge/corner variants, overlapping decoration, material blends, transition pieces, and texture variation to hide modular construction.
- Transition pieces between terrain surfaces/materials and between modules are welcome. Keep collision and diggable masks authored separately from decorative art so continuous-looking edges remain predictable.
- Preserve the core carve/clear -> route fluid -> intake -> hose -> extinguish interaction, with optional collectibles and routing tradeoffs.

Confirmed dummy-intake direction: use subtle but learnable visual clues before water reaches them, plus clear feedback when water enters. Avoid indistinguishable traps. Candidate clues include a missing droplet emblem, a damaged fitting, or a different collar; the exact visual treatment and dummy behavior remain to be selected.

The generated concept intentionally shows a solved main channel plus optional untouched areas to communicate intake/hose operation; actual levels start with the main route unexcavated.
