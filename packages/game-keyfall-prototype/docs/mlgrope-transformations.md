# mlgrope adaptation notes

Source pin for both rooms: `emersion/mlgrope` commit
`1c398f18dfb5977fb1f7fcb8a671584a102f406a`. Only the two named CSV files and
the repository `LICENSE` were consulted. The source CSVs are not shipped or
used at runtime.

## Campaign 1 — The Draft Gallery

Source: `levels/0.csv`.

The source's single suspended play object, nearby bubble, fan, loose balls,
goal, star, and spike informed the object relationships. The room is rebuilt on
Keyfall's 560x800 stage: the suspended object becomes the one brass key; the fan
becomes a clearly marked continuous gallery draft; loose balls become one
theatrical counterweight and a safe bumper; the spike becomes a low, forgiving
reset strip; and the star becomes three optional clue tickets placed away from
the zero-ticket route. Coordinates, scale, cord construction, forces, names,
prose, goal position, ticket path, and solution timing were reauthored. Popping
the bubble before cutting yields a broad, stable completion path through the
draft across tested clear-to-cut delays from 256 to 1,504 milliseconds. Leaving
the bubble intact instead creates a longer rafter loop through all three tickets;
popping it at the checked-in return cue banks all three at the lock. A mistake
resets only this attempt.

## Campaign 2 — The Bubble Column

Source: `levels/1.csv`.

The source's vertical stack of balls, two bubbles, stars, and a low goal informed
the column idea. The adaptation replaces the stack with one cord-suspended brass
key, two widely spaced reusable Keyfall bubbles, two off-axis theatrical
counterweights, three optional clue tickets, and a safe lock above a separately
telegraphed reset strip. The portrait geometry and complete interaction sequence
are newly tuned for articulated cords: clear both bubbles, then cut the cord for
a readable straight descent. Cutting immediately is the checked-in zero-ticket
route; waiting for the suspended key's later leftward phase before cutting sends
it through all three offset tickets and still into the lock. Both routes are
fixed-step replay fixtures under production tuning.
